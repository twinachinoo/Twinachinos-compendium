# shared content
Some new abilities, spells, or feats will reference new content. If you see a new term not in D&D's base conditions, refer to here.

### spell (and other) buffs
These effects help to introduce some more variety into otherwise "subpar" spell selections, such as infamous options like *acid arrow* or *hellish rebuke* - awfully mediocre options for the spell slot, now compete based on unique effects instead of damage.
:
other items and class abilities might be affected by these conditions, but I'm not too sure yet. 

#### affected spells (buffs)
Each new condition or effect will come with a section like this to detail which spells have been granted with new bonuses.

### coated with acid
This effect becomes permenant against a *construct*-type creature, or at least, until they are targeted by a *lesser* restoration or greater healing spell.
:
**coated with acid:** If the creature wears metal armor, they gain a -1 penalty to AC granted by the armor. If the creature wields metal weapons, they gain a -1 penalty to attack and damage rolls. 
:
**coated with vicious acid:** If the creature wears metal armor, they gain a -2 penalty to AC granted by the armor. If the creature wields metal weapons, they gain a -2 penalty to attack and damage rolls with it. 
:
**coated with ichor:** If the creature wears metal armor, they gain a -3 penalty to AC granted by the armor. If the creature wields metal weapons, they gain a -3 penalty to attack and damage rolls with it. 

#### affected spells (buffs)
*acid arrow* now also inflicts *coated with acid* on a failed save.
:
*vitriolic sphere* now also inflicts *coated with vicious acid* on a failed save.

\column

### bleeding
When a creature is bleeding, they cannot regain hitpoints, and when they are targeted by a healing spell, the spell removes this condition instead of granting any hitpoints.
:
The condition also ends if they take a short or long rest and expend hit dice.



## class unique effects

### shadowflame
A unique variant of the burning condition avaliable to the warlock, it progressively grows in power as you gain levels, and cannot be put out with water. It can still be put out by the conventional method of spending your action to drop prone, to roll around and end the burning condition.
:
When a creature is burning with shadowflame, on the start of each of its turns, it takes necrotic damage equal to **1d4+half your warlock level**. This means it scales into late game with relevancy, compared to the meager 1d4 of burning.

#### affected spells (buffs)
Creatures that fail the save against *arms of hadar* or *hellish rebuke* also causes the target to *begin burning with shadowflame* on a failed save.

### Hollow projection
A unique and powerful buff option avaliable exclusively to the artificer. This effectively lets them put up an additional "infusion effect" for the whole party, as the spells are non-concentration and last for 8 whole hours; it comes at the cost of a high level spell slot.
:
For balance reasons, only a single hollow projection can exist at one time.

# spell design and specification

Spells introduced here are generally designed to suit the campaign's theme as unique abilities. They **do not have to be cast as spells**. Consider these unique abilities that creatures, traps, or others can utilize.

### core design principle
Many of these ar designed to give more content for underrepresented damage types, effects, or classes. Consider the *artificer*, for example, would be a fantastic fit to introduce more **lightning damage**, and electricity-themed spells. Or for example, the deeply underrepresented *acid* damage type in spells.

### ranger....
Ranger, 2014 or 2024, has long felt disjointed between their spellcasting, skill proficiencies, and martial ability. I've decided to lean into what martial-style spells they do have to create a few interesting options that both deal good damage and offer thematic and useful effects. 
:
they're also Non-concentration, for the most part, meaning rangers might be able to do something whilst they have hunter's mark going.

*1st level transmutation (Ranger)* 

**Casting Time:** :: Bonus Action
**Range:**        :: Self
**Components:**   :: S
**Duration:**     :: 1 minute

You suffuse an piece of ammunition with the elemental energy of water that can freeze creatures in place.
:
When you hit a creature with an attack roll using this ammunition within, In addition to the normal effect of the attack, they take 1d8 cold damage, and must make a strength saving throw or have their speed reduced to 0 and become *vunerable* to lightning damage until the end of your next turn.

*1st level transmutation (Ranger, artificer)*

**Casting Time:** :: Bonus Action
**Range:**        :: Self
**Components:**   :: S

You suffuse up to four pieces of ammunition with magical potency, allowing them to seek out targets. When you miss with an arcane arrow, you can use your reaction to reroll the attack against a different target within 30 feet.
:
**At Higher Levels.** When you cast this spell using a spell slot of 2nd level or higher, the increase to attack and damage rolls increases to by one, and you select an additional four pieces of ammunition for each slot level above 2nd.

# martials like good feats
Title says it all. Sharpshooter, great weapon master, polearm master. It often does feel like these are mandatory in order to make a build work. But when I play a class that is meant to be good at dealing damage, I want to deal a lot of damage, And do it a way I enjoy. These feats are not going to be as intensely powerful as GWM or PM, but should still offer strength and open new build paths.

### feat scaling?
All feats are just suddenly unlocked by level 4. What about making more powerful feats gated behind higher levels? You know - basic progression? There will be some soft requirements in place here. 

#### proficiency with martial weapons
A simple yet important gate for certain powerful feats, which should not be avaliable to already powerful blaster builds. It does make them avaliable to gishes, though. I'm a swords bard fan.

#### feat at level 6 for rogues
I would highly reccomend using the optional house rule where the rogue gains an additional ASI at level 6, if you wish to fully utilize the power of these feats. This is a noticeable power increase to the class. (see: chapter 3: house rules)

## rogue-style feats

### Lethal accuracy
*prerequisite: level 4+, proficiency with martial weapons*
:
**Ability Score Increase.** Increase your dexterity, constitution, or Strength score by 1, to a maximum of 20.
:
**Improved critical.** Your uncanny accuracy strikes true on critical weak points. The number you need to score a critical hit on an weapon attack roll is reduced by 1.


### assassin's apprentice
*prerequisite: level 4+, not the rogue class*
:
**cunning action**: You gain one option between cunning action: dash, hide, or disengage.
:
**sneak attack**: Once per turn, you can deal an extra 1d6 damage to one creature you hit with an attack if you have advantage on the attack roll. The attack must use a finesse or a ranged weapon.
:
You don't need advantage on the attack roll if another enemy of the target is within 5 feet of it, and you don't have disadvantage on the attack roll. 


### Dextrous
*prerequisite: level 4+, the rogue, ranger, monk or bard class*
:
**Ability Score Increase.** Increase your dexterity or intelligence score by 1, to a maximum of 20.
:
Once per turn, you can give yourself an additional bonus action to use on that turn. You can use this feature a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus per long rest.

### Critical disaster
*prerequisite: level 8+, the lethal accuracy feat*
:
You gain the following benefits:
:
**Ability Score Increase.** Increase your dexterity, constitution, or Strength score by 1, to a maximum of 20.
:
**Improved critical.** The number you need to score a critical hit on a weapon attack roll is further reduced by 1.
:
**lethal critical**. Once per turn when you score a critical hit, you roll your weapon's damage die an additional time and add it to the total amount of damage dealt.

## monk / druid feats

### Martial artist
*prerequisite: level 8+, the monk, fighter, barbarian or bard class*
:
**Ability Score Increase.** Increase your dexterity, strength or constitution score by 1, to a maximum of 20.

Your martial arts have turned your bare hands into deadly weapons. Your unarmed attacks ignore resistance to bludgeoning damage.

*prerequisite: level 4+, not the monk class*

*prerequisite: level 8+, the monk, ranger, barbarian or druid class*
:
**Ability Score Increase.** Increase your dexterity, strength or wisdom score by 1, to a maximum of 20.
:
You forgo crude steel to attune your body to the elemental energies of the natural realm. Your melee weapon or unarmed strikes can deal fire, acid, lightning or cold damage in place of its original damage type.

*prerequisite: level 8+, the monk, ranger, barbarian or druid class*
:
**Ability Score Increase.** Increase your dexterity, strength or wisdom score by 1, to a maximum of 20.

You empower your attacks with your own vicious venom that courses through your body. Once per turn, when you hit with an unarmed strike, or another type of natural weapon (claws, tail, breath weapon), you deal additional poison damage equal to your proficiency bonus, and the creature must make a constitution saving throw or become *poisoned*.

## warlock feats
warlock's lack of a third spell slot until level 11 has been a pain point for the class since it's infancy. However, directly adding a third slot at level 5 or 7 is a significant power spike, and is ill advised. 
:
Secondly, the wait between levels 5 to 11 for a third eldritch blast strike is long and arduous. These feats strike a happy medium, introducing genuine oppertunity cost to achieve more power.

### Pact magician
*prerequisite: level 8+, the spellcasting or the pact magic feature.*
:
**Ability Score Increase.** Increase your intelligence, wisdom or charisma score by 1, to a maximum of 20.
:
You gain a pact magic spell slot of 3rd level, and you can use this spell slot to cast any spells you have prepared. If you already have the pact magic feature, you instead simply gain an additional spell slot.

### fractalizing magic
*prerequisite: level 8+, the eldritch blast cantrip.*
:
**Ability Score Increase.** Increase your intelligence, wisdom or charisma score by 1, to a maximum of 20.
:
when you cast *eldritch blast*, you can fire an additional miniature copy that deals a d6 of force damage instead of a d10.

# New invocations
*warlock is already a decent blaster, but I just really, really like eldritch blast. I've designed a specific pact boon and some invocations to enable that playstyle further without relying on a subclass.*
:

# usage of house rules
These are a collection of rules gathered over time playing with multiple different groups, where we adressed some common sore points, whether it be in 2014 or 2024. Ultimately, the impelementation of these rules depends on the DM's discretion. 

### Patch weaknessess over adding strength
They serve more to *patch weaknesses* than to pump more power into a class. D&D is not a balanced game; let's accept that early, and look to ensure a class's mechanical chassis does not get in the way of roleplay, storytelling, and combat.

### multiple options
Many different house rules have been drawn from different campaigns, and ultimately, depend on an agreement between DM and player. If a class has multiple unique options, it is generally advised you only select only 1-2 to implement.

### Optimizations
The design behind these features primarily focus on raising a class's baseline, not pushing their ceiling. This is to aid "subpar" builds still feel strong and capable. However, Optimizers might discover combinations I am not aware of. 



## design commentary
#### warlock
2024 made warlock less centered on EB, but it's still **the** damaging cantrip for most builds. Rather awful for the "tax" on your invocations and cantrip slots to something the class is known for, even if it's iconic. 
:
The new beshadowed blast feature is much more impactful on a 2014 warlock than a 2024 warlock, as the latter learns more invocations and spells. 

#### bard
These are very much campaign specific house rules, due to the length of time between long rests in my campaigns, bard simply replenishes all their bardic inspiration on a short rest, and regains the song of rest feature. These features mean the bard can still use their signiature abilities between long rests, and they actually *help* recovery during short rests with SoR.
:
Don't kill me. I know swords bard exists.

\column

#### rogue
There's a lot to say about rogue. Rogue damage is considered fine for T(ier) 1 play, levels 1-4. T2 (level 5+) is when they really begin to fall off without extra attack. 2024 hasn't helped much - cunning action is it's own can of worms.
:
Are any of these new features individually as good as extra attack? No. I don't think rogues should get Extra attack, their current 2024 identity still focuses on a single, precise strike. 
:
On the level 6 ASI rule, I'm aware some rogue players like to go all in on damage. Even if not, other rogues may not want a second set of expertises, or simply, just not at level 6. Expertise at level 9 the same as bard and ranger in 2024.
:
Criticality is a unique custom option, giving the class some on-demand nova capacity, but it moves away from the idea of the consistent martial striker they were in 2014. Mathematically, it comes out to about the same as d8 sneak attack - it's about giving more options over just doing strictly more damage.
:
:
***I would not reccomend using the crit rate boost if the custom feats are enabled**; there are crit rate granting feats, and crit rate is a steep exponential damage increase to unbalanced levels.*
:
**Implementing all the new features here will break balance. I reccomend the level 6 optional ASI + 1 combat feature.**

#### fighter

Fighter's great in 24, this buff is mainly aimed at helping weaker builds just...do more damage - PB damage once per turn doesn't help the sweaty crossbow expert a lot, but it does make sword and board feel a bit nicer. They're not going to beat a paladin's crit smite in nova, but the fighter is the king of sustained damage.
:
The alternative, and a personal favourite, is implementing maneuvers and superiority dice into the base class. This is a very noticeable power boost, so I've chosen to space out the dice and maneuver gains at important "spike" levels - which happen to be when half casters get their higher level spell slots. A smooth parallel, and fighter healthy number of total maneuvers and dice (5 at level 17, on a short rest).*

## rogue

### level 5: improved critical
As a rogue, you trade frequency for precision in your attacks. At level 5, Your attack rolls can score a Critical Hit on a roll of 19 or 20 on the d20.
:
At level 11, this range increases to 18-20.

### level 5: sneak attack die [in use]
At level 5, your sneak attacks become more decisive and deadly. Your sneak attack die increase from a d6 to a d8.

### level 6: ASI [in use]
At level 6, instead of gaining expertise in two skills, you may instead select an ASI or feat. You gain expertise in two skills at level 9 instead.

### level 5: Criticality
at level 5, You select precision over frequency. You gain a number of charges of **criticality** equal to your proficiency bonus. You regain all charges on a long rest.
:
When you deal sneak attack damage, you can expend a charge of criticality to maximise the damage of your sneak attack die and your weapon.
